# Glossary

**4 A's** — the AI-literacy competencies students must build: Application, Authenticity, Accountability, Agency (Ch 4)

**Adaptive testing** — tuning item difficulty and content in real time from learner responses (Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 9)

**AI-enabled vs AI-powered** — merely permitting AI in a traditional task, vs building AI into the task so the interaction is the assessed evidence (Ch 4)

**AI-driven grade inflation** — marks rising because AI polishes work, until grades stop discriminating (Ch 2)

**Algorithmic bias** — training-data inequities reproduced in grades and feedback, narrowing which answers count as acceptable (Ch 3, Ch 9, Ch 10)

**Application gap** — AI's measurable weakness at Bloom's application level, because application is local and situation-bound (Ch 10)

**Authentic assessment** — a task anchored in this cohort's context, real problems, and the student's own experience, with a reflective element (Ch 2)

**Balanced AI Integration** — four tenets: clear guidelines, process over product, encouraging critical thinking, feedback and reflection (Ch 4)

**Barely-passable submission problem** — students who would have failed now scrape through, so the signal that triggered remediation never fires (Ch 2)

**Black-box problem** — a grading decision that cannot be explained, so the student cannot learn from it and nobody can audit it (Ch 3, Ch 7)

**Centaur vs Cyborg** — a visible human/AI seam (grade the handoff decisions) vs continuous iteration (grade the transcript) (Ch 6)

**Competency-based assessment** — advancing students on demonstrated mastery; the check against fundamentals atrophying (Ch 6)

**Context-dependent item set** — one stimulus (passage, chart, diagram) followed by a series of items; the best proctorable higher-order format (Ch 6)

**Credential–competence gap** — degrees overstating capability, shifting training cost onto employers (Ch 2)

**Critical evaluation of AI output** — interrogating a machine answer for incompleteness, bias, and unstated assumptions; the new core graded act (Ch 10, Ch 6)

**De-skilling / atrophy risk** — teachers over-delegating planning and grading, or students never acquiring what they delegated (Ch 3, Ch 6)

**Detection evasion** — generative output leaves no forensic trace, which is why policy and task design, not detectors, are the lever (Ch 9, Ch 6)

**Detector false positives** — detectors systematically flag plain prose, penalising beginning writers, non-native speakers, and grammar-tool users (Ch 6)

**Difficulty gradient** — model success rate falls as item difficulty and operation count rise (Ch 11)

**Digital divide** — unequal device, connectivity, and tool access that turns any AI benefit into a stratifying force (Ch 3, Ch 2, Ch 9)

**Evolution document** — a record of initial AI suggestions, the student's reasoning, and why the final choice diverged (Ch 4)

**Extended Resources page** — a disclosure appendix on every submission listing every resource used and *how* it was used (Ch 1)

**False sense of achievement** — believing one has mastered material when one has only mastered operating the tool (Ch 2)

**Feed Up / Feed Back / Feed Forward** — Hattie & Timperley's three feedback questions: where am I going, how am I going, where to next (Ch 8)

**Five Can'ts** — can't ignore, prevent, detect, dissuade, or engineer around GenAI; therefore redesign (Ch 6)

**Flow** — matched challenge, clear goals, immediate feedback; the design spec an adaptive system is trying to hit (Ch 8)

**Formative vs summative** — assessment *for* learning, feeding back mid-stream, vs assessment *of* learning at the endpoint (Ch 8, Ch 3)

**Generative feedback** — answering a student hypothesis with a counter-hypothesis or unconsidered variable rather than a verdict (Ch 7)

**Hallucination** — output that is untrue or unreasonable, making learner intuition about plausibility a required skill (Ch 11, Ch 4)

**Higher-order thinking** — analysis, synthesis, evaluation, creation, problem-solving, judgment (Ch 10, Ch 7, Ch 6)

**Inquiry-Based Learning (IBL)** — students driving learning by posing questions, investigating, and constructing knowledge (Ch 7)

**Interaction log** — the full student–AI transcript, used as primary assessable evidence (Ch 4, Ch 6)

**Learning analytics** — reading interaction data to surface misconceptions, cohort difficulty hotspots, and at-risk students for early intervention (Ch 9, Ch 3)

**Modality equity problem** — online students have AI access and proctored in-person students do not, producing incommensurable grades (Ch 6)

**Multiple representations** — diagrams, graphs, number lines, manipulatives, and critically the *relationships among* them (Ch 11)

**Normalization** — an innovation becoming ordinary practice; requires student, teacher, and institutional expectations to converge (Ch 5)

**Novice advantage** — generative AI's gains are largest for the least skilled (~14% average, ~34% for novices) (Ch 4)

**Obfuscation tooling** — paraphrasers and style-matchers that defeat both plagiarism and AI detection (Ch 6)

**Procedural vs conceptual explanation** — the correct step sequence vs why the rule holds; AI is fluent in the first, unreliable in the second (Ch 11)

**Process over product** — grading the stages of composing, and crediting competence step by step, rather than scoring a terminal artifact (Ch 1, Ch 10, Ch 4, Ch 6)

**Prompting as an assessable skill** — response quality is bounded by prompt specificity, so prompt construction and constraint refinement are part of the performance (Ch 10, Ch 11)

**Query-formulation barrier** — students' difficulty composing useful follow-ups; the top self-reported obstacle at every performance level (Ch 8)

**RAG** — grounding AI feedback in a curated authoritative corpus, updatable without retraining; graph-structured variants map understanding across topics (Ch 8)

**Selection bias in tool adoption** — high performers adopt at higher rates, confounding tool effect with prior ability (Ch 8)

**Self-Determination Theory** — autonomy, competence, relatedness; the diagnostic for a well-built but unused tool (Ch 8)

**Sense making** — the fundamentals base that lets a learner judge whether a generated output is plausible (Ch 11)

**Show-your-work effect** — model accuracy on word problems rises substantially when working is required (Ch 11)

**SLMN** — "single, uniform (standard) language and modality"; the narrow convention set most writing assignments quietly enforce, resting on a myth of linguistic homogeneity (Ch 1)

**Trajectory** — the shape of a learning curve across staged, incremental evidence; the unit of assessment that replaces the artifact (Ch 2, Ch 7, Ch 9, Ch 10)

**Twelve-challenge audit** — the pre-adoption checklist: privacy, bias, pedagogy, cheating, human dimension, transparency, access, cost, ethics, training, skill limits, motivation (Ch 3)

**Two-axis assessment shift** — redesign moves fall on task type (toward reasoning and representation) or modality (toward in-class and verbal) (Ch 11)

**Viva / oral defence** — live justification of submitted work; the strongest AI-resistant instrument, and the most time-expensive (Ch 2, Ch 11)

**Writing apprehension** — fear of being red-penned, which drives outsourcing; the motive behind most AI substitution (Ch 1, Ch 4)
